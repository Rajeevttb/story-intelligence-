The Man Who Built 9 Lakh Nests For A Vanishing Bird
Conservation • #WorldSparrowDay • India VERIFIED — Source + IG + Visual
14
trust /15

Today is World Sparrow Day — a day started by an Indian. Rakesh Khatri was a documentary filmmaker who left his career in 2007 to save sparrows. He founded Eco Roots Foundation and has since built over 9 lakh (900,000+) nests using bamboo, jute, cloth and cotton. He trained 2,000 women across Bihar, UP, and Haryana to make nests from water hyacinths through "Operation Jal Sparsh." The birds adopted 70% of his nests. Sparrow populations in Indian cities have dropped 50-80% in two decades. One man decided that was unacceptable.

Today is the 168th death anniversary of Rani Avantibai Lodhi — the Queen of Ramgarh who raised an army of 4,000, defeated British Deputy Commissioner Waddington, and when neighbouring kings refused to fight, sent them bangles with the message: "If you have duty towards our motherland, raise your swords. Otherwise wear these and hide." On March 20, 1858, facing certain defeat, she pierced herself with her own sword at Sukhi-Talaiya rather than be captured. India Post issued stamps in her memory. NCERT added her to textbooks in 2012. But most Indians still don't know her name.

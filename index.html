In March 1974, village elder Gaura Devi confronted gun-wielding loggers in Uttarakhand's Reni village. She hugged a tree. Other women followed. The loggers left. The Chipko Movement forced PM Indira Gandhi to issue a 15-year ban on Himalayan tree felling in 1980. Today, 1.6 billion people globally depend on forests for survival. The world loses 10 million hectares every year — the size of Iceland. Deforestation accounts for 12-18% of global carbon emissions. India's Forest Rights Act (2006) has recognised 2.2 million individual forest rights and 102,889 community forest rights across 6.8 million hectares. Yet implementation remains hollow — in Karnataka, Adivasi families are still evicted from land the law says is theirs. The hug worked. The paperwork didn't.

69 People Died for Walking Without a Card — 66 Years Ago Today
South Africa / India • Racial Discrimination / Caste Mar 21 — Intl Day Against Racism
15
trust /15

March 21 is the International Day for the Elimination of Racial Discrimination. The date marks the Sharpeville Massacre. On March 21, 1960, approximately 5,000 Black South Africans gathered outside a police station in Sharpeville, refusing to carry "pass books" — identity documents that dictated where they could live, work, and travel. At 1:30 PM, police fired 1,344 rounds without warning. 69 killed. 180 wounded. Many shot in the back while fleeing. The UN designated this date to confront racial discrimination globally. In India, the conversation intersects with caste — movements against caste discrimination have drawn from anti-apartheid struggles. Dalit feminists have found shared language with Black feminists. The pass book and the caste certificate serve eerily similar functions.
